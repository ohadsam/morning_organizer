# User guide / מדריך למשתמש

> The in-app guide (Guide tab) is the same text. Keep both in sync - `tests/unit/misc.test.js` checks the section titles. Current version: see `web/version.json`.

## Quick start
Open Routines and tap Start on a sample routine, or create your own with “New routine”. Each routine is a list of tasks. Every task has a duration (minutes + seconds) and an optional time estimate.

## Building a routine
Tap a routine to edit it. Drag the ⋮⋮ handle (or use the arrows / Alt+arrow keys) to reorder tasks. Time estimate = your own guess of the real time; leave it empty to use the duration. Defaults live in Settings. The star adds a routine to Favorites - those also appear in the Android home-screen widget.

## Running a routine
Start begins the first task. The ring counts down; at 10 seconds left you get a short beep and when time is up a longer one (Settings: beep, vibrate, both, silent). “Done ✓” (or ticking the checkbox) moves on. Pause, +1 min, Skip and Finish are always one tap away. Time keeps counting as “over” until you tap Done. The summary shows start time, planned vs. estimated vs. actual time and each task. A run in progress survives closing the app.

## Statistics and insights
Stats shows every routine you finished: a chart of total time per run (actual, planned, estimate), averages and a per-task table. After 3 runs you get insights: tasks that always finish early (shorten) or late (extend), and the net effect on the total frame. Tap Apply to update the duration.

## Start alarm
Wake-up alarms: in the editor switch on “Remind me to start”, choose time and days. The first alarm has its own sound, volume, vibration, length and message; “Add another alarm” (or “Add N more alarms every M minutes”) adds more, each after a gap you choose and each with its own sound, volume, message and optional spoken voice - for example 7:00, 7:15 and 7:20. After the last alarm choose “Offer to start” (a Start button) or “Start the routine automatically”. Android: real alarms that ring even when the app is closed; on a locked phone the app opens by itself, otherwise tap the notification (its Start button always starts). Browser: only while the page is open.

## Unplanned interruptions
Unplanned interruptions: during a routine tap ⚡ Unplanned and pick what happened (toilet, spilled drink, forgot something... or your own words). The routine clock stops and that time is counted on its own - not as task time and not as a pause - then tap “Back to the routine”. The summary and Stats show how often and how long. When the same interruption keeps coming back in a routine (in at least 3 of the last runs) a card offers to add it to the routine as a real task, or to look closer: a few guiding questions (why, what happens before, one thing to try) are saved in your journal and the app keeps counting to show whether it changes.

## Sharing
Share creates a code and a link. Send it with the system share sheet (Bluetooth, Nearby Share, WhatsApp), copy it, or write it to an NFC tag. Import accepts a code, link, text file or an NFC tag. There is no server: everything is transferred directly and checked on arrival.

## Encouragement, stars and screen
Settings → Name and My buddy: the app cheers you on by name with cheerful messages, emojis and little animations (turn off or have them read aloud in Settings). Finish a routine to earn up to 3 stars (all done within the plan = 3). Finishing at least one routine a day builds a 🔥 streak shown on the home screen. Pause is the big button next to Done: the clock stops and the paused time is shown in the summary. The ☀️ chip on the home screen (or Settings) keeps the screen on while the app is open so you can tap progress any time.

## Mindful coach and voice
Settings → About you: add your name, age and gender - messages and tips then speak to you (Hebrew uses the right masculine/feminine forms) in a tone that fits your age. Tap 💡 Tip (next to Pause) or the 💡 on any task to get a short CBT / mindfulness idea: breathing, grounding, checking worried thoughts, letting thoughts pass, kindness to yourself, focus, time pressure. Tap "Another tip" for a new one. Switch on "Automatic tips" and the coach offers a tip when a task runs over, you are 3+ minutes behind, or you stayed paused for 2 minutes. Under Settings → Voice choose whether encouragement and tips are text only or also spoken automatically (same tone, name and gender forms), then pick a voice and a style (deep, high, robot, chipmunk, giant, sleepy, superhero, fairy). Styles change pitch and speed of your phone's voice; install a Hebrew voice in the phone's Text-to-speech settings if none is found. The coach gives general wellbeing ideas, not treatment. If worry feels overwhelming, talk to a trusted adult or a professional.

## Moving data between the app and the website
The website (PWA) and the Android app keep their data separately. To move routines, history and settings between them: Settings → Move data → Save backup file / Copy sync code / Send…, then on the other one → Import backup (file, or paste the code). Merge keeps the newest version of every routine and adds missing history; it never deletes. Replace makes this device an exact copy of the backup. Tick "Also import settings" to bring your name, voice and preferences too.

## Leave time, pace and accessibility
In the editor set "Leave by": the run shows whether you will make it (spare time or late) and warns 10 and 5 minutes before. "Suggest start time" sets the start alarm so you finish exactly in time. Settings → Pace: Quick shortens every task by 30%; Calm hides warnings and red "over" states so there is no time pressure. "Say the next task out loud" reads "Now: …" at each task. Settings → Accessibility: text size, color-blind friendly colors, high contrast and screen-reader announcements (TalkBack). Tasks can have a sticker or your own photo (tap the task icon).

## Family: parent and child phones
Open the Family tab. On the parent's phone tap "Add a child" and give the invitation to the child's phone: tap the two phones together (NFC, Android app), write it to an NFC tag, send it by Bluetooth / Nearby Share / a messenger, or copy it. On the child's phone tap "Join a parent", paste the invitation and send the reply back; the parent approves. An invitation works once and expires after 24 hours. It can also be shown as a QR code for the other phone to scan. Once linked, the parent sees the child's stars, streak, recent routines and what they are doing right now; sends routines, cheers and a reward goal (20 stars = ice cream, or "5 on-time routines this week") and gets an alert when the child is running late (over a live link). When the goal is reached the child celebrates and the parent is told. There is no server: messages are encrypted and travel directly. With "Connect live (Wi-Fi)" (both phones on the same network) or the Android app's Bluetooth link (pair the phones first) updates are instant; otherwise each message is a code you pass on by NFC tag, share sheet or copy-paste.

## Progress widget (Android)
Add the "Routine progress" widget from the home screen: it shows the routine in progress, the current task, a live countdown and a progress bar, and opens the app when tapped.

## Simple mode, accent color and settings
Settings → Appearance → "Simple mode for kids" shows only the routines with big Start buttons. Everything else (editing, settings, statistics, family) is locked behind a quick grown-up question for 10 minutes at a time. Pick an accent color for the whole app, plus light/dark theme, text size, color-blind friendly colors and high contrast. Settings are grouped into sections you can collapse; the app remembers which are open.

## Weekly summary, history and examples
Stats shows "This week": routines finished, stars, on-time count, active days and whether you got faster than last week, plus a 5-week calendar heat-map. "Share summary" sends a short text; "Export history (CSV)" saves every run for a spreadsheet. Deleting a routine offers "Undo" for a few seconds. Settings → "Add more example routines" adds bedtime, homework and after-school routines.

## People, avatars and levels
Several people can share one phone: tap the avatar on the home screen (or Settings → About you) to switch person or add one. Each person has their own name, age, avatar, routines history, stars, streak, badges and parent link. Pick an avatar: animated characters (they blink, wave, cheer, dance and sleep as you go - fox, cat and panda from the start, more unlock as you level up), an emoji, your own photo, or your own animated GIF (up to 160 KB). Photos and GIFs stay on your phone. Stars raise your level (shown on the home screen); higher levels unlock new characters.

## Days, gear and achievements
In the editor, "Schedule" sets which days a routine is for (school days, weekend or any days); the home screen lists today's routines first. "Gear to remember" adds things for certain days (PE kit, swimming bag): they appear as a checklist when you start and are counted in the summary. Badges (first routine, streaks, on time, early bird, speedy, comeback…) are earned automatically; new ones are celebrated on the summary screen and all of them are in Stats → Achievements.

## Android app and widget
Routine card widget: add “Routine card” from the widgets list. It shows one saved routine with its tasks, durations, days, leave-by time and gear count; tap it (or ▶ Start) to run it in the app with all its saved settings. Use ‹ › on the widget to show a different routine (each widget remembers its own). While that routine runs the card becomes a live countdown with progress.

Settings → Download Android app always gives the latest APK. Allow “install unknown apps” for your browser once. Long-press the home screen → Widgets → SonRise to start a favourite routine in one tap.

## Helpers: calm, focus, journal and rewards
Choose what you want help with: Home → “What would you like help with?” (or Settings → Helpers). Pick areas such as focus, calm, time sense, remembering, motivation or habits - the app switches on matching tools. A parent can choose this for a child from the Family tab (🎯). Every tool can still be switched on or off on its own.

Calm corner (🌬️): breathing patterns, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding and a body scan, 1-3 minutes, with a gentle vibration cue. During a routine it pauses the clock while you breathe. Focus sprints split long tasks into short sprints with a 30-second movement break; the parking lot (🅿️) saves a stray thought so you can get back to the task; the next-task heads-up shows what comes next; the pie timer shows time as a filling circle; the mood faces after a routine feed a mood insight in Stats.

Journal (📓): notes, voice notes (🎙️ - recorded during a task too), thought checks, “3 good things” and parked thoughts. It is stored only on this device and is not part of backups or sharing. Password (optional): Journal → Password. It is stored as a salted hash and the entries are encrypted; adding a note never needs the password. Forgot it? Answer your two security questions to set a new one. If you forget both, entries cannot be opened - you can only erase them.

Speech to text (optional): in a note tap “Dictate” (or record - in a browser a suggested transcript appears while you speak). It is only a suggestion: check and edit it. In the Android app it uses the phone’s own recognizer; in a browser the browser’s speech service may send audio to its maker, so the app asks first. Reward menu (🎁): spend stars on rewards from a menu you or a parent set up; a parent is told and can mark it as given.

## Easier editing, wording and extras
Editing tasks: tap the time on a task to open the time sheet (quick times, ±1m/±15s buttons, exact minutes and seconds), or use the − / + buttons next to it. The ☰ menu on each task lets you add a task below, duplicate, move up/down/to the top/to the bottom, change the picture or remove it; dragging by the handle still works. If-then plans (editor → “If-then plans”) show as a card when the routine starts: “If I want to scroll my phone, then it goes in the drawer”. Settings → Appearance → “Timer look” switches the ring to a pie, a bar or a sand clock. Young children: with an age under 12 the whole app uses lighter, more fun wording (Settings → Appearance → Wording to force it on or off). Android: the alarm notification has a 5-minute Snooze, and a “Calm corner” widget opens breathing in one tap. Parents see a weekly digest (slowest steps, repeated interruptions) on each child’s card.

## Templates
Templates: save tasks and routines you use again and again. In a routine’s editor tap “Save as template” (💾) for the whole routine, or “Save task as template” in a task’s ☰ menu. “Add from templates” opens big tiles of your saved tasks and ready-made ideas (morning, evening, school, home) - search, filter, and tap as many as you like. On the home screen “From a template” starts a new routine from one of your templates or a ready-made idea. Home → Templates manages them: use, rename, edit time, share, delete (with Undo). Templates are included in “Back up all data” and sync codes.

## Goals
Goals (Goal - Plan - Do - Check): Home → Goals. Write one goal that matters to you (“be ready on time without reminders”), optionally tie it to a routine, and choose up to three strategies that fit you - for time, planning, remembering, attention or organising - or write your own. Do: the plan appears as a card when you start that routine. Check: after the routine tell the app how it worked (a face and a few words); the app shows how many routines were on time since you began and whether your check-ins are getting better, and prompts a weekly check-in. A parent can suggest a goal from the Family tab (“Suggest a goal”), and “Sit with me” shows the child that the parent is with them during a routine. Inspired by goal-based, strategy-focused approaches such as Cog-Fun (Hebrew University occupational therapy). It is a self-help tool and does not replace an occupational therapist or other professional.

## Alarms screen
Alarms screen: Home → Alarms lists every routine with its wake-up alarm - switch it on or off, see the schedule (e.g. 07:00 → 07:15 → 07:20) and edit the whole chain. Each routine card also shows its alarm. After an update a one-time “New in…” sheet points to new features.

## Task feelings and help
Feelings and help on every task: while you do a task, rate how hard it is with the icons (🪶 very easy … 🥵 very hard). “Why is it hard?” opens a note (type or record) with that task attached - what is hard and which thoughts came up. “A kind thought” and “Relax” give a different suggestion each time, worded for your age: kind thoughts (effort, self-compassion, reframing, starting small) and short exercises (breathing, squeezing and releasing, shaking out, five senses, butterfly hug and more); breathing exercises can open the Calm corner. Everything is saved with the run. Stats → “Where is it hard?” ranks tasks by average difficulty, flags the ones that are both hard and slow, and counts how often notes, kind thoughts and relaxation were used - so you can see where more help is needed.

## Reports, PDF and e-mail
Reports, PDF and e-mail: Stats → “Export & send” (or 📄 next to any run in a routine’s history, or on the summary screen) opens a designed report with a summary, insights in plain words, charts over time, an activity calendar, per-routine and per-task tables, difficulty, interruptions, mood and goals - for one run or for the last 7/30/90 days or all time, for all routines or one. “Save as PDF” makes a real PDF file; “E-mail the report” opens your e-mail app with the PDF attached (Android app, or phones that support sharing files) - in a browser the PDF is downloaded and the mail opens with a summary, and you attach the file. Print and copy-summary are there too. Reports are made on this device only.

## Smart times and extra steps
Smart times and extra steps: in a routine’s editor, “Smart times” suggests a duration for every task - from your own past runs when there are some (typical time, a little more if it usually runs over), otherwise a sensible estimate for that kind of task adjusted for age and the help you chose (focus, calm, time, remembering). A small “about 8m” hint also appears under a task name. “Smart extra steps” proposes short in-between steps that fit the help you chose - for anxiety a calming breath at the start and a calm check before something long or hard; for attention picking one thing to start, a movement break after a long stretch, clearing the desk before homework; for memory saying the steps aloud and a last check. Each suggestion shows why; you tick what you want. A parent can choose “Suggest for” a linked child. Steps added this way offer a one-tap exercise when they come up in the run.

## Cog-Fun tools, version and HTML reports
More Cog-Fun-inspired tools: Stats → “How close are my guesses?” compares the time you guessed for tasks with what really happened and says whether you tend to guess short or long and whether you are getting closer. In a goal’s check-in, tick which strategies you used - the goal then shows which one went best. Parents get a daily “Ask, don’t tell” guiding question in the Family tab. Alarms are on by default for new and ready-made routines (Home → Alarms), the version number is shown at the bottom of Home and in Settings, and “What’s new” lists what changed. Reports can also be saved as one interactive HTML file with animations (tabs for 7/30/90 days and all time, numbers counting up, bars and charts drawing themselves, collapsible sections; it works offline and prints well).

## Looking back
Looking back (retrospective): after a run, tap “Look back” on its summary (or 🔄 in a routine’s history, or the prompt on Home - the run with the most going on comes first, and you can do it days later). First choose how: guided (one step at a time, you can stop and continue later) or free (everything on one page); for this run or for the routine’s last 5 runs (the usual picture); the whole thing or only some parts (how it felt, tasks, what went well, what to change) - the rest can be done in another look-back, and you can do as many look-backs as you like for the same run. For each task choose went well, got stuck or not now; where it got stuck pick why (hard to start, distractions, too big or unclear, tired, could not find something, worries, not enough time). The app then suggests concrete changes to this routine - more time, a helper step before it, splitting a task, an earlier alarm, a gear item, planning for an interruption, a goal with a plan - and you apply the ones you tick in one tap (each can be undone). After you apply changes the app compares the routine’s next runs with the earlier ones (on time, over/under the plan, difficulty, stuck tasks, interruptions) and tells you whether it helped, got worse or made no clear change - waiting for 3 runs first, and then a card on Home tells you. Home → Looking back shows what to focus on (tasks that keep getting stuck or were set aside), what worked and what did not, what gets in the way, drafts to finish, and automatic conclusions; Settings → Helpers lets you set your usual style. Results are part of the reports (PDF and HTML) and backups.

## Alarms, quiet days and the toolbar
Home has one toolbar - New, Wizard, Plan, Calm, Track and Alarms. Plan, Calm and Track open a short list under the bar; Alarms pauses all alarms (until tomorrow, for a week, or until you switch them on). Every routine card has an alarm switch and a ⏰ sticker when it has an alarm. In an alarm, choose the type of ring (tap a tile to hear it; in the Android app also a sound from your phone) and keep "Quiet on holidays and vacations" on to stay silent on Jewish holidays and school vacations, including the summer. Home shows the Hebrew date and today’s holiday.

## Calm corner: body scans and guided imagery
Choose Breathing, 5-4-3-2-1, Body scan or Guided imagery. Body scan has four kinds: quick, full (head to toe), tense and release, and for sleep. Guided imagery has 11 short journeys - the four elements (earth, air, water, fire), a safe place, waves, forest, mountain, leaves on a stream, a worry balloon, a worry box, warm light, a floating cloud and a kind helper - shown one step at a time and, if you like, read aloud. These are relaxation exercises, not treatment.

## Situation log (EIER)
A way to understand hard moments, from CBT. Four short questions - Event (what happened), Interpretation (what I told myself), Emotion (what I felt, how strong) and Response (what I did) - plus an optional step for another way to see it. Write it, dictate it or record it. Find it in Journal → Reflect, in the task help card (Log this moment), at the end of a calm exercise, or by search. After three logs the journal summarises your patterns (feelings, thinking traps, what eases things). It is private to this device and is a self-help overview, not a diagnosis.

## Goals inside routines and the wizard
A goal can be linked to a routine (the routine card shows a 🎯 chip, the editor has a Goal section, and the run screen shows the plan). In the wizard, the Goal step lets you pick or create a goal, and the goal adds its strategies as the first steps of the new routine. In the review step you can rename steps, change their time, remove steps and add your own before creating. From any goal, "Build a routine for this goal" starts the wizard.

## Start over, cancel or delete a run
A routine in progress is never a dead end. On the run screen open More → Change the plan: Start over begins again from the first task, Cancel this run leaves without saving anything. The same two buttons are on the Home "continue" card. Nothing from a cancelled or restarted run goes into your history. A finished run can be deleted from its summary screen (Delete this run) and brought back with Undo.

## What the app is for, people and voice commands
In Settings → What the app is for choose a way of working (CBT, Cog-Fun) and/or what is hard (attention, anxiety, getting organised, motivation) and an age; the app then shows only what fits, and "Show everything" brings it all back. Each person has their own settings, look and view: switch from the little menu at the top, or manage everyone in Settings → People (with several people the app can ask who is using it at the start). At the top of Settings are a search box and a jump bar; under Look choose a skin, easy-read text or your own colour; the welcome screens can be opened again from Settings. During a routine, tap Listen for commands and say a short phrase (done, add time, pause, resume, skip, journal, calm corner, remind me the step, how much time is left, stop - which always asks first). It keeps listening through all steps; the ? button and Settings → Voice commands list every command.

## Sharing between people, special tasks, points and moving routines
Who sees a routine: with several people on the device, a routine's "Who sees this routine" section lets you choose everyone or only some people. Special tasks: in the editor tap "Add a special task" and pick by topic or search (journal, thought parking, body scan, guided imagery, relaxation, voice note...); on the run screen the task shows one button that opens the tool. Points: Settings → Points and awards (off by default): automatic or your own points per step, a bonus for finishing early, a little less for finishing late (never below 0, never in the calm pace, can be switched off), awards (medal, coin, trophy...) won by finishing before the time, a podium with fireworks at the end and a points chart plus running total in Statistics. Moving routines: Home → Plan → Share and move routines: copy to another person on the device, save or send as a file, bring in from a file (imported alarms come in switched off).

## Kids view and full view
Next to the "who is using the app" chip at the top there is a button: Kids view switches to the kids' view (only routines and a big Start) in one tap. In the kids' view the top bar shows only Full view, which asks a quick grown-up question before it brings everything back. The choice is kept per person.

## NLP tools and calm animations
In Settings → What the app is for you can choose NLP as a way of working, next to CBT and Cog-Fun. Home → Calm → NLP tools lists six short guided exercises: a clear outcome, another way to see it (reframing), a calming anchor, precise words (finding big words like "always" and "can't"), points of view, and mental rehearsal. Each asks a few questions, one at a time, and can save your answers to the journal. They are also special tasks you can add to a routine; on the run screen the task opens its exercise. NLP is a popular set of coaching techniques, but the research evidence for it is limited and mixed: these are gentle self-reflection exercises, not therapy and not a promise of results. Settings → Accessibility → Calm animations turns off movement such as fireworks, confetti and bouncing.

## Avatar studio and My avatars
Open the avatar picker (Settings → About you → Avatar, or when editing a person) and tap "Build my own", or open "My avatars". In the studio a live preview at the top shows your avatar; try its animations (rest, wave, cheer, dance, sleep, surprised, think) and feelings (happy, calm, sad, worried, annoyed, tired, excited, proud). Choose a tab: face, hair, eyes and mouth, clothes, extras, a friend and background, or colours. Options with a lock unlock at higher levels. "Surprise me" picks a random avatar. Give it a name and tap Save, or "Use as my avatar". My avatars lists your saved designs: use, edit, duplicate, share or delete (undo is right there). Share sends a short code through WhatsApp, e-mail or the clipboard; "Add from a code" shows the avatar before adding it. Saved avatars are part of the backup file.

## Updates and sharing the app
The web app updates itself - when you open it, and every hour - and switches to the new version on its own when nothing is running; if you are in the middle of something it asks first. The Android app updates from the latest download. To share SonRise open Settings → App → Share (or search "share"): choose the Android app or the web app, and send it by WhatsApp, e-mail, the share sheet or a copied link.

## Search, wizard and suggestions
Tap 🔍 (bottom bar or Home, or press Ctrl+K) and type what you want, e.g. "alarm", "voice" or a routine name - tap a result to go straight there. The wizard (Home → Help me build one, or search "wizard") asks who it is for, how old, what helps, which routine and when, then builds the routine with fitting times and optional calm / focus breaks. Now and then Home shows one gentle suggestion; choose Try it, Not now or Don’t show this again (Settings can switch suggestions off). Screens are kept calm: look under "More" for the rest of the tools.

## Voices
Voices: Settings → Helpers/Voice. Pick a character: everyday voices (woman, man, girl, boy, grandma, grandpa), speaking styles (coach, calm guide, storyteller, radio host, teacher) or fun characters (cat, dog, lion, frog, pirate, dinosaur, robot, fairy) - animals say their sound first (“meow”). Each character picks a matching installed voice (male or female, the most natural one first, other characters use another voice when your device has more than one), speaks in phrases with natural pauses and rising or falling intonation at a calm human pace, and never reads emoji or abbreviations. You can still choose a specific device voice (★ marks natural/neural ones), set the speaking pace, and a wording style (casual, warm and traditional, polite and formal) that adds a short opener now and then. Choosing anything plays a sample. How human it sounds depends mostly on the voices installed on your device - the settings include short steps for getting better Hebrew and English voices on Android, iPhone and Windows. Characters change voice, pace, pauses and wording; they are not recordings of real people and cannot copy real accents. In the Android app you can also pick the voice engine (Google, Samsung, or a free offline neural engine such as SherpaTTS or Hayai TTS that you install separately) and then a voice from it.

## Privacy
All data stays on your device. Use Settings → Back up all data to save or move it.
